Research Overview

How are heavy and superheavy nuclei formed?

The research addresses a central question in theoretical nuclear physics: which reaction mechanisms govern the formation of heavy and superheavy nuclei, and how reliably they can be predicted. Stochastic dynamics and systematic uncertainty analysis are the principal methods, supported by scientific computing.

Research Questions

Q1

How does a touching nuclear system fuse into a compound nucleus, rather than re-separate?

Above the Coulomb barrier, most heavy systems re-separate by quasifission. The probability of forming a compound nucleus is the least understood factor in superheavy-element synthesis, and it is studied here with Langevin (stochastic) dynamics.

Q2

How well can production cross sections for new elements be predicted?

Predictions for elements such as Z = 119 and Z = 120 guide experiments that run for months to observe a handful of events. The question is not only the predicted value but how far it can be trusted.

Fusion dynamics →

Q3

Which model parameters do experimental data actually constrain?

Reaction models carry free parameters that theory does not fix. Fitting them jointly to measured cross sections, and tracing the confidence region of the fit, shows which parameters are constrained by data and which are not.

Research Areas

01

Nuclear Reaction Dynamics

Langevin dynamics and stochastic simulation of heavy-ion reactions, from the colliding nuclei to a compound nucleus.

02

Superheavy-Element Synthesis

Fusion–evaporation cross sections for superheavy nuclei, including Z = 119 and Z = 120, connected to experimental studies of synthesis and decay at S3.

03

Computational Nuclear Physics

Development of KEWPIE3, a C++ code for fusion–evaporation reactions that computes capture, formation and survival for each reaction and energy.

04

Model Calibration

Joint fits of free model parameters to experimental cross sections, with Newton–Raphson refinement and Bayesian optimisation with Gaussian-process surrogates.

05

Uncertainty Quantification

Confidence corridors on predicted cross sections from profile-likelihood scans, and systematic comparison of competing model choices.
