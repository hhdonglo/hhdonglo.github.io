Research Overview

How are heavy and superheavy nuclei formed?

The research addresses a central question in theoretical nuclear physics: which reaction mechanisms govern the formation of heavy and superheavy nuclei, and how reliably they can be predicted. Stochastic dynamics and systematic uncertainty analysis are the principal methods, supported by scientific computing.

Research Areas

01

Nuclear Reaction Dynamics

Langevin dynamics and stochastic simulation of nuclear reactions, covering the pathway from the colliding nuclei to a superheavy element.

02

Heavy and Superheavy Elements

Production cross-sections for superheavy nuclei, including Z = 119 and Z = 120, are predicted with the KEWPIE2 and KEWPIE3 framework. The work connects to experimental studies of synthesis and decay at S3.

03

Fusion–Evaporation Reactions

Fusion–evaporation reactions and the probability of forming a compound nucleus, calculated with Langevin-based stochastic approaches.

04

Uncertainty Quantification

Bayesian optimisation and machine learning applied to estimate nuclear model parameters and their uncertainties, with systematic benchmarking of competing models.

Current project

KEWPIE3 development and validation

Objective: validate and publish KEWPIE3, the new version of the KEWPIE2 code for fusion-evaporation cross-section predictions in superheavy element synthesis.

Key question: how can the calculation of the compound nucleus formation probability be improved with Langevin-based stochastic approaches?
