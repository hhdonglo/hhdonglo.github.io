Research Overview

How are heavy and superheavy nuclei formed?

My research develops computational models of heavy-ion reactions to understand and predict the formation of heavy and superheavy nuclei. Stochastic simulation, uncertainty quantification and scientific computing provide the principal methods for this work.

Research Areas

01

Nuclear Reaction Dynamics (read more)

Langevin-based stochastic modelling of heavy-ion reactions. Fusion–evaporation reactions and the synthesis of superheavy elements are applications of the same description, with predictions for Z = 119 and Z = 120 made with KEWPIE3.

Current Projects

Current project

KEWPIE3 development and validation

Objective: validate and publish KEWPIE3, the refinement of the KEWPIE2 cascade code (which describes the survival of the excited nucleus) with an implemented and calibrated formation step, for fusion-evaporation cross-section predictions in superheavy element synthesis.

Key question: how can the calculation of the compound nucleus formation probability be improved with Langevin-based stochastic approaches?
