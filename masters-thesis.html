Master’s Thesis Summary

This dissertation describes the general framework of the symmetry conserving configuration mixing (SCCM) method, a beyond-mean-field approach based on the variational principle, and applies an axially symmetric version of it to study the structure of the even-even argon isotopes (A = 32 to 58). The same method is then used to explore the structure of 152,154Nd and 78Kr.

Key results

  • Argon isotopes: they exhibit collectivity with mixing of oblate and prolate shapes.
  • Magic numbers N = 20 and 28 persist along the isotopic chain, shown by higher first excitation energies than in the neighbouring isotopes.
  • Neutron sub-shell closures (2p1/2 and pf-shell) at 56Ar and 58Ar are indicated by the first excitation energies and the nuclear charge radius.
  • Binding energy per nucleon and charge radii are reproduced along the isotopic chain, and the axial GCM-SCCM results show good agreement with the experimental data.
  • 152,154Nd are found to be well deformed, with two distinct rotational bands: prolate and oblate shapes coexist.
  • 78Kr shows an admixture of oblate and prolate shapes. The moment of inertia of the first excited 0+ state is about one third of that of the ground state, which reinforces the shape coexistence.
