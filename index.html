About

Hope Donglo, PhD, is a Lecturer in the Department of Physics, College of Basic and Applied Sciences, University of Ghana, Legon, appointed in August 2026.

Hope develops theoretical models of heavy-ion reactions and superheavy element synthesis, employing stochastic methods, uncertainty quantification, and scientific computing to investigate reaction mechanisms and improve predictive nuclear theory.

Research

Research focus

The research develops computational models of heavy-ion reactions to understand and predict the formation of heavy and superheavy nuclei. Stochastic simulation, uncertainty quantification and scientific computing are the methods that support this central aim.

Research themes

1. Heavy-ion reaction dynamics

Reaction mechanisms are modelled with stochastic simulation, using Langevin dynamics. The approach covers the full pathway from the colliding nuclei to a superheavy element.

2. Fusion and superheavy-element formation

Fusion-evaporation reactions and the probability of forming a compound nucleus are calculated with Langevin-based approaches. Production cross-sections for superheavy nuclei, including Z = 119 and Z = 120, are predicted with the KEWPIE2 and KEWPIE3 framework.

3. Nuclear-model uncertainty and calibration

Bayesian optimisation and machine learning are applied to estimate nuclear model parameters and their uncertainties. Competing theoretical models are benchmarked against each other with systematic uncertainty analysis.

4. Scientific computing and stochastic simulation

Simulation pipelines are built and validated for high-performance computing environments, in modular C++ and Python. Earlier work applied numerical methods to nuclear structure, for example for argon isotopes.

Current research projects

KEWPIE3 Development and Validation

Objective: validate and publish KEWPIE3, the new version of the KEWPIE2 code for fusion-evaporation cross-section predictions in superheavy element synthesis.

Key question: how can the calculation of the compound nucleus formation probability be improved with Langevin-based stochastic approaches?

Fusion Dynamics and Compound Nucleus Formation

Objective: revisit the fusion dynamics in the fusion-by-diffusion model for fusion-evaporation production cross sections.

Output: manuscript in preparation (see Publications).

Uncertainty Analysis in Superheavy Element Synthesis

Objective: constrain the compound nucleus formation probability in superheavy element synthesis through systematic uncertainty analysis.

Output: manuscript in preparation (see Publications).

Overview

Scope, objective and weekly structure

Each week is taught in three contacts. Every lecture provides a slide deck, a tutorial sheet and a supplementary note. All files are PDF. Select the deck preview or a button on a card to open it. Each lecture also has twenty multiple-choice practice questions that run in the browser, for practice and not assessment.

Scope

Mechanics: lectures 1 to 8. Thermal physics: lectures 9 to 12.

Objective

To prepare students to succeed in the PHYS 143 examination through conceptual understanding, technical competence and application to unfamiliar problems.

Weekly routine: attend all three contacts, attempt the tutorial before the answers, review the supplementary note, and use the deck checklist to find gaps.

Contacts per week

  1. Contact 1: build the physical idea.
  2. Contact 2: develop the model and method.
  3. Contact 3: apply, integrate and transfer.

Research-informed learning

How foundational physics connects to current research

Vectors and nuclear reaction kinematics

The vector methods of Level 100 describe how momentum and energy are shared among the products of a nuclear reaction.

Statistics and uncertainty quantification

Measurement uncertainty, fitting and model comparison are the same ideas used to constrain the parameters of nuclear models.

Differential equations and reaction dynamics

Equations of motion with random forces, the Langevin equations, describe how a heavy nuclear system evolves during a reaction.

Programming and physical models

Scientific computing turns a physical model into numbers that can be compared with experiment, as in the KEWPIE codes.

Students

Undergraduate research supervision

Undergraduate students can take part in research projects in nuclear physics, mathematical modelling, scientific computing, data analysis and uncertainty quantification. Projects are built around the methods used in the research programme, adapted to the level of the student.

What a student might do

  • Build a small simulation in Python and test it against a known result
  • Fit a model to data and report the uncertainty of the fitted parameters
  • Solve the equations of motion of a simple system numerically
  • Analyse, plot and interpret a data set
