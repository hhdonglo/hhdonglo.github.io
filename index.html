At the Edge of the Periodic Table

Investigating the reaction dynamics of superheavy-element synthesis at the limits of nuclear stability

The synthesis of superheavy elements probes one of the central questions in modern physics: how heavy can matter become?

I develop theoretical and computational models of heavy-ion collisions to understand how nuclei fuse, survive fission, and populate the heaviest known region of the periodic table. My work combines nuclear reaction dynamics, uncertainty quantification and scientific computing to improve predictions for the creation of new elements.

Superheavy-Element Synthesis

Only a tiny fraction of heavy-ion collisions successfully leads to a surviving superheavy nucleus.

σER=σcap×PCN×Wsur\sigma_{\mathrm{ER}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{cap}} \times P_{\mathrm{CN}} \times W_{\mathrm{sur}}σER​=σcap​×PCN​×Wsur​

The synthesis probability is determined by three competing processes: capture, compound-nucleus formation and survival against fission.
